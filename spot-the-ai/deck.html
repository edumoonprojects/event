How to play without phones

Teams, paper and a pen. That’s all.

Make teams of 3 or 4

Each team picks a name and writes it at the top of a sheet of paper.

Write your answer

Write the letter (A, B, C or D). For “tap the line” questions, write the line number and the letter.

Points

100 for each correct answer. Line questions: 100 for the line + 100 for the reason. The last round counts double.

Speak up

Speaking moments earn bonus points. The host decides who gets them.

Before Round 3 · Learn

Plot twist: nobody can reliably spot AI. Not even AI detectors.

Detectors make mistakes

They flag real human writing, especially simple, formal English

AI can sound human

Ask it to add typos and details, and it will

So the real skill is different

Not “who wrote this?” but “is this actually true and good?”

What this means for you

Keep your draftsVersion history proves your work
Judge content, not styleIs it correct? Is it specific?
Don’t accuse othersGuessing can be wrong

Before Round 4 · Learn

AI can be wrong and sound 100% sure.

It predicts words

It writes what sounds right, which is usually, not always, what is right

It can invent things

Facts, dates, quotes, links and even research papers that don’t exist

So: check what matters

Exams, assignments, health, money and anything you’ll repeat to others

How to check

Search itFind the fact on a trusted site
Check your notesTextbook, class notes, official docs
Ask it for a sourceThen open the source yourself

Before Round 5 · Learn

A better question gets a better answer.

Say who you are

“I’m a 2nd-year CSE student who knows basic C”

Say what you need

The task, the level, and what you’ve already tried

Say the format

“5 points”, “one example”, “a table”, “in simple English”

Weak → Strong

“Explain pointers”Too vague
“Explain pointers to a 2nd-year student who knows arrays, with one small C example”Clear level and format
Not happy?Follow up: “simpler”, “shorter”, “another example”

Use AI the Smart Way: 8 Rules

Use it to learn, not to skip learning

Ask it to explain, then practise yourself.

Check every fact

Search every source it gives

Your real details and experiences make writing yours.

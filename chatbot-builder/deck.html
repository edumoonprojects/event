How to play without phones

Teams, paper and a pen. That’s all.

Make teams of 3 or 4

Each team picks a name and writes it at the top of a sheet of paper.

Write your answer

Write the letter (A, B, C or D). For “tap the line” questions, write the line number and the letter.

Points

100 for each correct answer. Line questions: 100 for the line + 100 for the reason. The last round counts double.

Speak up

Speaking moments earn bonus points. The host decides who gets them.

Before Round 3 · Learn

Good replies are short, correct and suggest the next step.

Short

Two or three lines on a phone screen

Buttons

Offer the next likely question: “How to pay?”, “Late fee?”

Ask when needed

“Which year are you in?” before giving year-specific dates

A good flow

User“Fee last date?”
Bot“Which year? [1st] [2nd] [3rd] [4th]”
Bot“1st year: 15 Dec. [How to pay] [Late fee]”

Plan for when the bot does not understand.

Fallback

“Sorry, I didn’t get that. Try: Fees, Exams, Hostel.”

Do not guess

A wrong answer is worse than “I don’t know”

Human handoff

A clear way to reach a real person

Always hand off

Health or safetyShow helplines at once
Complaints and disputesA person must decide
Repeated failuresAfter 2 fallbacks, offer a human

Before Round 5 · Learn

Two kinds of chatbots: rule-based and AI-powered.

Rule-based

Fixed intents, fixed replies. Predictable and safe

AI-powered

Understands free text and writes replies. Flexible, but can make things up

Grounding

Make AI answer only from official college documents

Choose by risk

Fee dates, rulesExact answers: rules or grounded AI
Study tips, explanationsAI is fine
AlwaysShow the source for important answers

Chatbot Design Checklist

Screenshot this. Use it to design your first bot.

Purpose

One clear job: “Answer fee, exam and hostel questions for first years.”

Scope

List what it will NOT do, so it does not pretend.

Intents

Each thing users want: fee_due_date, exam_timetable, hostel_rules.

Examples

10 to 20 different ways people ask for each intent, including Tenglish.

Replies

Short answers with buttons for the next likely question.

Fallback

“Sorry, I didn’t get that. Try: Fees, Exams, Hostel.”

Handoff

A clear way to reach a real person for hard or sensitive cases.

Test with real users

Watch 10 students use it. Fix the questions it failed.
